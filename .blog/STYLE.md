# Writing style

Write from a real question, experiment, or decision. Preserve uncertainty that remains useful.

Prefer this movement when it reflects the source conversation:

```text
initial mental model → contradiction → evidence → revised understanding → open question
```

Use first person for Ryan's own reasoning, but never invent a thought, experience, result, preference, or conclusion. Added background should be framed as background, not as Ryan's discovery.

Keep technical claims precise. A small inspected example is usually more valuable than a broad generic introduction. Use headings to expose the argument, not to manufacture drama. Avoid repetitive summaries, inflated conclusions, tutorial filler, and excessive lists.

Notes may remain narrow and unfinished. Articles should earn their length by connecting ideas. Project logs should preserve concrete observations and decisions close to when they happened.

Before a draft is ready, verify:

- the original question is visible;
- the source supports every personal statement;
- observations, explanations, and inferences are distinguishable;
- code, math, and links are accurate;
- unresolved questions have not been edited away;
- the title and description say something specific.
